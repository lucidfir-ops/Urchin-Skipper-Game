URCHIN SKIPPER — CREW BANTER AND RELATIONSHIPS
23 September 2026 · Writing proposal, requested by the designer

The living Bible remains the authority. Only the small surface-bubble first
pass below is implemented. The following longer relationship arc is a proposal.

IMPLEMENTED FIRST PASS
Surfacing produces a short-lived cartoon bubble: a thumbs-up for a useful
sample, thumbs-down for poor/empty catch, or a pause sign for fatigue/a requested
surface interval. Authored text has a 65-simulation-second per-person cooldown.
Opinions use the bag just picked and the diver’s actual state; they reveal no
hidden nearby ground. A bubble follows a visible surfaced person and disappears
on descent. Ada, Milo, Nell and Roy have distinct lines; other crew have a small
neutral set. Save/reload preserves the cooldown.

WHAT MAKES A RELATIONSHIP WORTH REMEMBERING
The skipper should recognize a person through how they work. Ada remembers a
useful drift and appreciates orderly recoveries. Milo likes moving quickly but
can learn when waiting is the right decision. Nell likes good records. Roy’s
bravado should never imply that his hidden disregard of limits is safe advice.
Existing biographies govern additional characterization; do not replace them
with arbitrary new backstories.

Proposed next layer: keep a short, factual shared-history ledger per person.
Examples: first full bag together; returning before fatigue became severe;
recovering an injured colleague; a week of steady earnings; being left waiting
at the surface; revisiting a mark they reported. Let one moment be mentioned
at the next natural pause, not immediately after every input. Never claim that
the skipper caused a bad outcome unless the simulation establishes it.

THREE LEVELS OF WRITING
1. Work signals: a few words or a gesture at the surface. Fast to read, no
   mandatory dialogue. Catch opinion is provisional, based on one sample.
2. Familiarity: a name, remembered event or recurring joke during an offload
   or idle harbour moment. A second diver may answer. Keep exchanges under
   three short lines and give serious safety calls priority.
3. Trust: after repeated work together, occasional invitations to discuss the
   next day, a rest request, or a reason for staying with/leaving a crew. Any
   consequence should connect to existing wages, fatigue, availability or
   poaching mechanics, with visible warning before a consequential decision.

Example proposed exchange after a successful return to Ada’s recorded drift:
Ada: “Same bend in the kelp. Different tide.”
Milo: “And this time you believed the mark.”
These are future authored lines, not evidence that such an event happened.

CONTROLS AND PRESENTATION
Keep surface bubbles above the float without covering the hook cue. Offer
separate chatter visibility/volume settings before expanding dialogue. Future
speech should have the same subtitles and interruption rules. A work gesture
can remain when chatter is hidden. Do not require reading dialogue to steer.
Retain a few recent exchanges in a separate crew log; radio stays radio-only.

GUARDRAILS FOR A SECOND PASS
No romance meter, gift currency, alignment score or forced friendship quest.
Do not award invisible quality radar for liking a diver. Distinct working
preferences matter more than stat bonuses. Avoid a daily demand to hear every
line. Limit a substantial exchange to once per day and repeated topics across
several days; urgent safety communication has no narrative cooldown.

TRY IT WITH PLAYERS
After three trips, can the player identify two people by their voice and a
shared event? Do they understand why a request was made? Can they ignore the
dialogue and still operate safely? Does reload repeat a story or invent one?
Start with Ada and Milo, then expand only after those checks work.

SEPARATE WEATHER IDEA — NOT IMPLEMENTED
The designer suggested meaningful bad-weather lay days and possible sinking
on harbour passages above roughly 40 knots. Before adding a loss roll, make
the forecast threshold and departure warning explicit, tie risk to exposure,
hull condition, boat capability and the actual route, and let waiting/resting
be viable. Decide how a forecast change during a trip offers shelter or a
credible return choice. Existing wind, sea, visibility, fatigue and transit
penalties remain active. This update adds no random passage-sinking rule.
