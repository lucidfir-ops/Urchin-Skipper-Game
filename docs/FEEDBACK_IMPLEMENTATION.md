## Decisions

- The video supports silent fuel exhaustion, not a controller disconnect. Fuel, commanded helm and drive condition are separate facts. Shared engine-state logic gates propulsion and explains loss of power. A stopped engine preserves momentum, environmental drift and helm input. Pause exposes rescue near the top when stranded.
- Departure shows fuel aboard, outward/home passages, a 15-minute passage reserve and an approximate intermittent-throttle work allowance. Easy adds crew, condition and preparation advice; critical fuel/boat warnings remain in Realistic. These are actionable briefing items, not repeated confirmation prompts. Travel, fuel and return estimates use the same hull speed, loading and sea-state calculation. Corrected an existing return charge that still used prototype travel time.
- Crew receive 40% of their own landed catch value, before boat operating costs. This resolves the transcript's equal-catch example: each earns 20% of total sales. No guarantee or hidden mode-dependent economy. Catch quality, age and shipping loss affect attribution through the same offload calculation. Legacy unattributed catches are split fairly between the working crew; new recovered/rescued bags retain attribution. Settlement remains idempotent and fuel is paid only at bunkering.
- Career good-ground bag time is 45 seconds before crew differences and local search. Prototype laboratory recipes retain their 30-second reference for regression/history. Fatigue builds at .00028 per underwater simulation second, ordinary overnight recovery is .12, and a full harbour rest day adds .28 recovery. Early departure adds .06 fatigue once per trip; waiting cannot repeat it. Interest is fictional game balance at .2% per day, with no early-offload bonus. Existing physical ageing/shipping losses remain.
- Island Tender: $15,000, 4,000 lb, 17 kn, 15 L/h. Reef Runner: 5,000 lb, 14 kn, 25 L/h. Channel Master: 18 kn, 70 L/h. The unclear “E5” single-jet figure is interpreted as 35 L/h at 15 kn. Workhorse keeps 10 kn; reverse authority is modestly stronger. Coefficients live in `career-data.js` / `config.js`.
- The larger existing `thruster` hull is named Coastal Workhorse; existing ownership and 8,500 lb capacity are preserved. A $20,000 bow-thruster retrofit fits the original 5,000 lb Harbour Workhorse without enlarging it. New auxiliary tanks add 120 L, and carried fuel adds weight. Legacy percentage-sized tanks retain their installed volume and stored fuel on migration. Resale pays half the condition-adjusted hull/fittings value and half-price fuel; fit another boat first. Sold vessel histories are retained.
- Controller focus follows visible geometry. All menu buttons have stable choice IDs; the shared Back is controller reachable and replaces duplicate Back rows. Analogue and D-pad navigation share that geometry. LT/RT directly scrub forecasts, with forecast buttons available to D-pad users. Existing mappings, startup polling and hotplug are preserved. Remapping-device selection and prototype starting-ground selection retain their labelled left/right functions; they do not also move menu focus.
- Crew portraits select a candidate; separate berth buttons perform assignment. Unavailable portraits remain inspectable with reasons. Equipment displays fitted stations separately from its catalogue. Corrected harbour rest/dock indices that opened unrelated screens.
- Forecast labels show career day and local clock, preserving the continuous tide epoch. HUD layout separates weather/instruments from reveal diagnostics; the ground artwork and kelp remain intact.

- Career recovery ignores repeated presses on the active operation. Automatic range/speed pauses preserve progress and resume when safe. The original laboratory keeps its manual pause behavior; menu/focus/disconnect pausing is preserved. This is the bounded interpretation of the ambiguous spoken “pause” complaint.
- DFO now explains the routine call, recovery/boarding requirements, 18 game-minute check, permitted helm movement and the harbour alternative. Officers Morgan and Singh provide a brief result; no assessment occurs merely for choosing a harbour inspection. Injured crew cues distinguish a medical return from a fatality/sinking emergency.
- Frank's optional harbour lesson starts with boat positioning and neutral momentum, then sounder/drop choice and port recovery. Departure advice repeats only the essentials. Three arrival approaches choose safe positions along the correct entrance; no reef teleport. Hull clearance is checked for alternate positions.
- Remembered reef outlines are approximate five-metre sketches with dated reports, not live stock truth. Existing stock depletion/recruitment remains authoritative. Local exposure affects both wind and seas; physical cabin wind load adds yaw and visible whitecaps follow the same wind. Quiet days remain quiet.
